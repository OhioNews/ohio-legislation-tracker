💡 How to Testify at a Committee Hearing

Never testified before? It's easier than you think! Your voice matters in the legislative process.

What is Testimony?

Testimony is your chance to tell legislators how a bill will affect you, your family, or your community. You don't need to be an expert - just speak from your experience.

Who Can Testify?

Any Ohio resident can testify on any bill before a committee. You don't need special credentials or permission.

Two Ways to Testify:

1. Written Testimony - Submit a written statement (easier for first-timers)

  • Write 1-2 pages explaining your position
  • Include your name, city, and which bill you're addressing
  • Submit via the witness slip (linked below each hearing)
  • Typically due 24 hours before the hearing

2. Oral Testimony - Speak in person at the hearing (more impact!)

  • Sign up using the witness slip
  • Arrive early to the hearing location
  • You'll get 3-5 minutes to speak
  • Legislators may ask you questions

Tips for Effective Testimony:

  • Be personal: Share YOUR story, not statistics
  • Be specific: Explain exactly how the bill affects you
  • Be brief: Make your main point in the first 30 seconds
  • Be respectful: Even if you disagree strongly
  • State your position: Clearly say if you support or oppose the bill

What to Include:

  • Your name and where you live
  • The bill number you're testifying on
  • Whether you support or oppose it
  • How it affects you personally
  • What you want legislators to do

Remember: Your testimony doesn't need to be perfect. Legislators want to hear from real people affected by their decisions. Your experience matters!
